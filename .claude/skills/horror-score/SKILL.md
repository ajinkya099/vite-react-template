# Horror score, motif and tension-music director

## Compose to story, not adjectives
1. Identify the protagonist's human emotional motif and threat's distinct acoustic signature. Keep them recognizable even when transformed.
2. Make a cue map *before* animation: exact timeline, narrative function, entry/exit, emotional curve, musical space and dialogue ducking.
3. Alternate scored and unscored spans. Remove music when environmental sound or silence carries greater fear.
4. For sustained tension, select one stable textural idea (sub-bass pulse, detuned bowed texture, breathing rhythm, brittle high tone) and vary density rather than stacking everything.
5. For a scare, establish what expectation is violated. Build impact with picture, timing and transient design, not maximum music volume alone.
6. Give aftermath music or silence time to register loss, disbelief or a new terrible fact.
## Timing sheet
`CUE | 00:SS.mmm start | end | theme | texture | intensity 0-5 subjective | sync marks | under dialogue? | transition`.
## Music licensing
Use original or properly licensed stems; never assume internet music can be used commercially. Preserve stems separately for rebalancing.
## Acceptance
Music supports performance and makes information legible; its absence is an intentional creative choice; no unlicensed copy of a recognizable protected composition.
