# Fearful blocking, staging and spatial choreography

## Block before framing
1. Draw plan view and specify coordinates or stable left/right/near/far anchors for door, window, furniture, exits, light, characters and threat.
2. Record entry state: body center, stance, facing, eyeline, free/occupied hands, prop positions and distance to danger.
3. Give each character a goal, obstacle and tactical action: look under the table, cover the bell, signal a friend, secure the latch.
4. Make the character choose between conflicting needs so stillness feels purposeful, not frozen.
5. Place the threat relative to eyeline; show whether viewer, character or neither can see it.
6. Document contact, force, recoil and exit state; every object that moves stays changed in the next shot.
7. Choose coverage (wide, OTS, insert, reaction) only after action and axis are clear.
## Escalation examples
Threat gets closer without obvious travel; familiar ally moves to an impossible location; an emergency exit is visibly blocked by an earlier choice; a noise forces the protagonist to expose their hiding place.
## Performance specificity
`hesitates at switch -> reaches -> hears breathing behind -> freezes mid-reach -> pulls hand to chest -> takes one step back`.
## Outputs
Floorplan; character/action lanes; eyelines; prop handoff ledger; entrance/exit poses; hazards and effects timing.
## Reject if
The characters teleport between cuts, pass through props, always look at camera, or repeat walking without changing the situation.
